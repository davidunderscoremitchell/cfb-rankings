The Mitchell Metrics reward what teams have actually done on the field: wins and losses against their real schedule, with every opponent judged by a performance-based Strength rating. The rankings are calculated using three key numbers:

Strength is a measure of how well a team plays. It is calculated using margin of victory and statistical analysis from thousands of previous games to answer the question: How hard is this team to beat?

Résumé is the theoretical record that best explains a team's results. Measured against the team's Strength-adjusted opponents, it answers the question: What record would we expect this team to have?

PAL is the low end of the Résumé's 95% likelihood interval. It favors consistent results and makes the statement: This team has "proven at least" this good. PAL is the headline ranking of the Mitchell Metrics.

A record-based ranking ignores opponent quality. A strength-based ranking measures performance over outcomes. The Mitchell Metrics take both into consideration to create a balanced assessment of what each team has achieved on the field.

Résumé, PAL, and Strength are calculated as a winning percentage and displayed as a record: Résumé and PAL are adjusted by games played, and Strength is adjusted for a full 12-game season. How the rankings work →
